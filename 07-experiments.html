8.2 Stable parameter range for K_mem, K_tar and σ_p0

Here, we are explaining a combinations of parameter Kmem​, tether force parameter Ktar​ and myogenic stress scale σp0​ that ensures that the pressuirzed wall stay stable and doesn’t drift downstream under the pulsatile flow.
Question. For which combinations of membrane spring Kmem​, tether Ktar​ and myogenic stress scale σp0​ does the pressurized wall stay stable (no blow-up, no wrinkling, no buckling, uniform inflation) and not drift downstream under the pulsatile flow?

Chosen configuration

Ktar​=2.34×109, Kmem​=1.5×1010, Nb​=512, Δt=10−4 s, Pset​=2 Pa, σp0​ calibrated for ε=0.2 → offset 0.29 µm, wall strain ±0.1 %, D/D0​=1.2001 (prediction 1.2000).

Parameter ranges and what happens outside them

(Flow on with pulsatile amplitude 0.6, Pset​=2 Pa unless stated. Each “outside the range” example changes one parameter of the chosen configuration; runs in Simulation_Results/Blood_vessel_v2/2026-10-17/.)

1. Axial tether Ktar​ — range 4.5×108≲Ktar​≲1×1010 µm/s²

  • What it does: holds each wall node at its target point against the wall shear of the flow.
  • Too small → downstream drift. The wall slides downstream until the tether carries the shear: offset ≈6.7×108/Ktar​ µm (right panel). Above 1 % D0​ (1.5 µm) for Ktar​<4.5×108. Example Ktar​=2.34×107 (run_001): the whole wall shifts ≈ 29 µm and slides between 12 and 45 µm with every pulse. It is a static offset — the wall does not keep creeping — and the wall itself stays intact.
  • Too large → blow-up. The tether force is explicit, so a too-stiff spring overshoots in one time step and the error grows every step. Measured ceiling at Δt=10−4 s: stable at 1.0×1010, blow-up at 1.3×1010 (8.4 ms), 1.7×1010 (4.0 ms), 2.34×1010 (2.4 ms, run_005: the whole wall translates axially, ×2.5 per step). The ceiling is numerical and scales as 1/Δt.

2. Membrane spring Kmem​ — range Kmem​≳6×109 µm/s² (no upper limit found up to 2.5×1010)

  • What it does: keeps neighbouring wall nodes at their rest spacing (the stiffness of the wall along its length).
  • Too small → wrinkling, then buckling. A disturbance starts at the open ends of the wall chain (the periodic junction), travels upstream and grows exponentially; the wall crumples locally (strain > ±15 %), the diameter becomes non-uniform, and finally the vessel kinks sideways and the lumen pressure leaks. Kmem​=4×109 (run_002): strain limit at 5.1 s, kink at 7.4 s. Kmem​=2.5×109 (run_003): 4.0 s and 6.1 s — softer walls fail sooner.
  • A very stiff axial tether (7.4×109) suppresses the wrinkling down to Kmem​=1.5×109 (6 s runs).
  • Caveat: near this bound the wall strain was still growing at the end of the 6 s runs (e-folding 0.75–1.4 s), and Kmem​=4×109, classed “wrinkled” at 6 s, buckles at 7.4 s. The bound needs confirmation with ≥ 20–30 s runs.

3. Myogenic stress scale σp0​ — any value tested (σp0​/(Ktar​D0​)=2.7×10−5–1.5×10−2, i.e. dilation ε= 10–35 %)

  • What it does: balances the transmural pressure; it is calibrated from Pset​ and the target dilation ε.
  • Effect: it only sets the equilibrium diameter (reached to 10−4); it does not affect stability. Larger dilations strain the wall slightly more.

4. Transmural pressure Pset​ — range ≲ 2.5 Pa (tested stable: 1, 2, 2.5 Pa)

  • Too large → pressure-induced buckling. The wall has no axial tension and no bending stiffness, so the pressure acts like an axial compressive load ≈ PD. At 10 Pa (run_004) the vessel inflates normally, then short sideways wrinkles (~12 along the vessel) grow within 0.6 s and it snaps into one large S-bend at 2.1 s. Afterwards the vessel leaves the fixed source band and the pressure control fails (see 05 Forces > 5.5 Lumen pressurization (source/sink)); the later “collapse” is an artifact of the method.
  • Physiological pressures (5–8 kPa) would need an axial pre-tension or bending stiffness, and a much smaller Δt.

5. Numerical parameters

  • Nb​=512 wall nodes (Δs0​≈Δx/2). With Nb​=200 (Δs0​≈1.3Δx) the flow excites a node-scale sawtooth that ends in buckling.
  • Δt=10−4 s. It sets the Ktar​ ceiling (∝ 1/Δt) and is needed by the stiff myogenic stress at higher pressures.
  • Radial tether Ky​=9.36×105 (not varied): deliberately weak, so that the radial position is decided by pressure, springs and myogenic stress.

8.3 Inflation and deflation with axial flow

Goal. With the chosen stable configuration (8.2: Nb​=512, Ktar​=2.34×109, Kmem​=1.5×1010) and the pulsatile flow on throughout, inflate the vessel with the transmural pressure and then change (i) the myogenic tone and (ii) the transmural pressure, to see how the vessel dilates and constricts.

(sim = mean D/D0​ over the last 0.4 s of each level; pred = force balance D/D0​=1+ln(1+(ekp​εD0​−1)/a)/(kp​D0​).)

  • Every level reaches the predicted equilibrium (error ≤ 6×10⁻⁴; the largest errors are for the softest wall, kp​=0.05, which relaxes most slowly within the 1.5 s steps). The response is reversible: back at a=1 the vessel returns to D/D0​=1.200.
  • kp​ sets the sensitivity: between tone 0.5 and 2 the diameter changes by 14 % (kp​=0.05), 8.7 % (kp​=0.1) and 4.6 % (kp​=0.2) of D0​ — a stiffer exponential law resists both dilation and constriction.
  • Each tone step produces a pressure transient (up to 4 Pa / down to 1 Pa): the massless wall changes its stress instantly, the source then drains or fills the lumen in ≈ 0.3–1 s, faster for larger kp​.
