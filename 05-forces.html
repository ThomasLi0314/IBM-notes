f=S[Ftet​+Fspr​+Fmyo​]+fdrive​(t),∇⋅u=s(x,t)

The force f is the force appeared in the fluid solver.

5.1 Tether (penalty) force

Anisotropic, with a separate stiffness along (x) and across (y) the wall, the same at every node:

Fktet​=(Kx​(Yk,x​−Xk,x​)Ky​(Yk,y​−Xk,y​)​),Kx​=2.34×109,Ky​=9.36×105 µm/s2
  • Details and history: 04 Boundary Configuration > 4.3 Tether stiffness (uniform, anisotropic).
  • Role: holds the wall axially against the mean wall shear (no downstream drift, no compressive collapse; the strong axial anchoring also delays pressure-induced buckling), while leaving it nearly free radially, where pressure, springs and myogenic stress set the diameter.

5.2 Spring (membrane) force

For segment k connecting nodes k→k+1 on the same wall:

Tk​=Kmem​(∣Xk+1​−Xk​∣−Δs0​),τk​=∣Xk+1​−Xk​∣Xk+1​−Xk​​ Fkspr​+=Tk​τk​,Fk+1spr​−=Tk​τk​
  • Open chain (no wrap-around).
  • 🟡**No pre-tension**: rest length = initial spacing, so a straight wall carries zero tension and has zero transverse stiffness to first order. The spring only resists axial stretching/compression.

5.3 Myogenic force (Michele et al. (2006), Eq. 18)

σp​(r)=⎩⎨⎧​σp0​(ekp​(r−r0​)−1),σp0​kp​(1−(r02​r2​)−3/2),​r≥r0​r<r0​​Fk,ymyo,top​=−σp​(rk​),Fk,ymyo,bot​=+σp​(rk​)
  • rk​ = local diameter, r0​=D0​. σp​>0 is contractile (walls pulled toward the centerline); σp​<0 pushes them apart.
  • Physical meaning: passive (elastic) stress of the smooth-muscle wall that stiffens exponentially with stretch — the stress that balances transmural pressure in a real vessel.
  • Enabled since 2026-10-13 to balance the transmural pressure of the pressurized lumen.

Per unit wall length. σp​ is a stress, so each node carries the wall length it represents, ℓk​=21​(Lk−1​+Lk​):

Fk,ymyo,top​=−σp​(rk​)Δs0​ℓk​​,Fk,ymyo,bot​=+σp​(rk​)Δs0​ℓk​​

(at the two coincident end nodes ℓk​ is half a segment, so the shared point is not counted twice).

Calibration. At equilibrium the spread myogenic force per unit length balances the transmural pressure, σp​(req​)Δs/Δs0​=Pset​. Choosing the target dilation req​−r0​=εD0​ gives

σp0​=ekp​εD0​−1Pset​Δs0​/Δs​≈3.0×108 μm2/s2(Pset​=2 Pa, ε=0.2, kp​=0.1)

Stimulation. The stress scale is multiplied by a user-defined activation, σp0​amyo​(t) (myo_activation). For a step change amyo​=a the new equilibrium is

D0​Deq​​=1+kp​D0​1​ln(1+aekp​εD0​−1​)

→ a=2 (stronger smooth-muscle tone): D/D0​=1.157; a=0.5 (relaxation, e.g. NO): D/D0​=1.245.

For comparison — Arthurs et al. (1998), Eq. 60 (Voigt viscoelastic model, used in v3 and in v2 before May 2026):

M(s,t)=KM​D0​D(s,t)​+γD0​∂t​D(s,t)​

5.4 Driving force

fdrive​(x,t)=Gg(t)ex​for every grid point strictly inside the lumen (yb​<y<yt​),

zero outside the vessel. Here the function g(t) is either a constant or a time oscilating function + a constant.

  • Time-modulation switch (initialize.m):
    • use_osc_drive = false → steady drive, g≡1;
    • use_osc_drive = true → g(t)= drive_profile(t), any user-supplied function handle. Default: g(t)=1+asin(2πt/T) with a=0.2, T=1 s (60 bpm; the drive never reverses for a<1).

5.5 Lumen pressurization (source/sink)

Following Arthurs et al. (1998, Eqs. 5–9). A closed periodic lumen conserves its area, so it cannot inflate unless fluid can enter it. A fluid source inside the lumen and a matching sink in the exterior exchange fluid at a pressure-controlled rate:

∇⋅u=s(x,t)=q(t)(win​(x)−wout​(x)),q(t)=Rsrc​Pset​−Plumen​(t)​,Plumen​=ij∑​pij​(win​−wout​)ij​ΔxΔy
  • win​: uniform over the rows ∣y−Ly​/2∣<37.5 µm (inside the lumen); wout​: uniform over the rows within 60 µm of y=0 (exterior). Each is normalized to ∑wΔxΔy=1, so the total source is zero, as a periodic field requires.
  • Like a short tube of resistance Rsrc​ connecting the lumen to a reservoir at Pset​: fluid flows in while Plumen​<Pset​ and back out if the wall squeezes the lumen (Plumen​>Pset​). At equilibrium q=0 and the transmural pressure equals Pset​; the lumen area is free to change.
  • Plumen​ is taken from the previous step’s pressure (explicit update).


Where fluid is added and removed. The source band sits on the lumen centerline, the sink band in the exterior next to y=0 (the same band also appears at the top because the domain is periodic in y). Fluid added in the lumen can only leave by pushing the walls outward; the exterior fluid they displace flows to the sink.
